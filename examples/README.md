# Examples

Nine notebooks that take the demo measurements in `../data` through the whole workflow of the paper:
characterise the resonator and the bias line, build the virtual device, simulate, compare. They run from this
folder (`jupyter lab` here, or `python ../scripts/run_examples.py` from the repository root to execute them
all) and write their figures, configuration files and recorded numbers to `results/`.

| Notebook | Paper | What you learn | Run time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `00_quickstart` | - | the device, the three simulations, a ramp through the bias line | < 1 min |
| `01_resonator_parameters` | Sec. IV B, Table I, Fig. 3 | cable delay, circle fits at every flux point, the flux-response fit, writing a configuration | 1 min |
| `02_all_channels` | Sec. IV B, Fig. 4 | a 32-channel survey from one sweep, a multi-channel configuration and simulation | 1.5 min |
| `03_flux_line_transfer_function` | Sec. IV C, Fig. 5, Table II | from a two-port of the bias line to its poles; the ramp amplitude from the source | < 1 min |
| `04_steady_state_s21` | Sec. V A, Fig. 6 | the virtual VNA against the measurement, residual metrics | < 1 min |
| `05_power_dependence` | Sec. V A, Fig. 7 | the RF dressing of the SQUID; fitting the line attenuation | 1 min |
| `06_flux_ramp_response` | Sec. V B, Figs. 8-10, Table III | folding IQ records, calibrating once at 1 kHz, predicting 27 other rates | 1 min |
| `07_square_pulse_response` | Sec. V C, Fig. 11, Table III | plateau calibration, pulse edges through the bias line | < 1 min |
| `08_demodulation_extension` | beyond the paper | a detector pulse through flux-ramp demodulation: what the reset transient costs, when discarding samples helps | 1 min |

## Numbers that flow between notebooks

`results/values.json` is a small store (`umux_cmt.io.ValueStore`) of the quantities one notebook records and
another reads: the line attenuation fitted in `05` (used to convert VNA and fridge-input powers to the power
at the chip in `01`, `02`, `04`, `06`, `07`), the ramp amplitude measured in `03` (used in `00`, `06`) and the reset
flux calibrated in `06` (used in `00`, `08`).
Every notebook reads them with the paper's value as a default, so any notebook runs on its own; run them in
order once to regenerate everything from scratch. The bias-line poles are not in the store: `00`, `06`, `07`
and `08` read them from `../data/flux_line_2026-10-02/flux_line_poles.npz`, the cached result of `03`, which
`03` checks against its own fit. The notebooks also record their headline results in the store, which is how
the test suite of the repository checks them against the paper.

## The generator scripts

Each notebook is generated by a script in `_build/` (with `nbformat`). Edit the script and run it, or edit the
notebook directly in Jupyter; the scripts exist so that the notebooks can be rebuilt clean, without outputs.
